# What the intron data supports putting on the wiki

*Written 2026-10-16 on branch `feature/intron-advisory`. Every number here comes from
`work/introns/figure_data.json`, generated by
`validation/experiments/intron_figure_data.py` and drawn by
`validation/experiments/intron_figures.py`. Regenerate both before quoting anything.*

## The one thing that changed since the last write-up

Every fidelity figure in `docs/intron_fidelity.md` was scored on **BsaI-HFv2**. Level 0 is a
**BbsI** reaction. `assembly_spec.py:43` calls scoring it the other way "a reaction that does
not happen", and Jan Vollmer said the same thing independently on 2026-10-16: BpiI and BsaI
belong to different cloning levels and never share a tube.

A second correction landed at the same time. Selection respected the published 500 nt exon
guideline but never the 350 nt oligo cap, so it had been recommending exons that cannot be
ordered. The orderable ceiling is 320 nt — 350 minus the 30 nt BbsI wrapper.

Both corrections push the same direction, and together they change the answer rather than
the decimal places.

| | old (BsaI-HFv2, no oligo cap) | now (BbsI-HF, 320 nt cap) |
|---|---:|---:|
| level 0 acceptor alone | 0.8282 | **0.7944** |
| 9S, intron-free | 0.8282 | **0.7928** |
| 9S, 3 introns | 0.8025 | **0.7234** |
| 14S | 0.751 (3 introns) | **0.1299** (4 forced) |
| 19S | 0.686 (4 introns) | **0.0812** (6 forced) |

## What the corrected data says

**No architecture clears our own floor, and no seed does either.** `FIDELITY_FLOOR = 0.75`;
the best result anywhere in the sweep is **0.7234**.

| arch | CDS nt | G\|G sites | route A clean | route B clean | introns | fidelity | vs floor |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 9S | 906 | 65 | 1 | 16 | 3 | **0.7234** | −0.0266 |
| 14S | 1371 | 89 | 1 | 25 | 4 | **0.1299** | −0.6201 |
| 19S | 1836 | 125 | 1 | 32 | 6 | **0.0812** | −0.6688 |

The intron counts for 14S and 19S are **not choices**. With exons capped at 320 nt, 1371 nt
needs at least five exons and 1836 nt at least six, so the junction count is forced upward by
the ordering constraint and fidelity falls with it. 9S is the only architecture where the
count is free, and it is the only one that lands anywhere near the floor.

Across five seeds on the same 9S target: **0.1615, 0.1615, 0.5963, 0.7234, 0.7234**. Seed 42
is not special after all — 2024 matches it — but three of five seeds produce placements that
are not usable, and none of the five clears 0.75.

### Where the fidelity actually goes

Scoring the 9S placement piece by piece, on BbsI-HF:

| | fidelity |
|---|---:|
| a perfect reaction | 1.0000 |
| the empty level 0 backbone, CTCA against CTCG | **0.7944** |
| plus the three intron junctions | **0.7234** |
| *(the three intron junctions with no backbone)* | *0.9106* |

The backbone costs **0.2056** before an intron exists, because the two strands that meet in
the tube differ by a single base. That leaves **0.0444** above our floor. Three intron
junctions cost **0.0710**.

The budget is spent before the introns arrive. Jan confirmed the vector is **pIM 046** and
is what the lab uses, so this is a fixed property of the backbone rather than a parameter
anyone can tune.

### What is true regardless of enzyme

These are sequence measurements and no matrix touches them:

- **rbcS2i1 passes every PPROPT synthesis rule unchanged.** GC 0.5793, zero of 96 windows
  outside the 0.35–0.65 band, longest homopolymer exactly 4, no BsaI/BbsI/SapI site on either
  strand, canonical `GT`…`AG`. Its worst window is 0.640 against a 0.650 ceiling — it fits
  with 0.010 to spare.
- **The stronger intron does not fit.** LHCBM1i2 (253 bp) was Baier's best of 33 native
  introns at about 8-fold against rbcS2i1's 5.5. Twelve of its 204 windows breach the
  ceiling, worst 0.680. The breach is interior, so no wrapper or pad choice moves it, and an
  intron cannot be recoded. rbcS2i1 is not a preference; it is the only one of the two that
  is orderable.
- **Three of six hosts cannot splice at all.** `e_coli`, `c_reinhardtii_chloroplast` and
  `n_tabacum_chloroplast` have no nuclear spliceosome. rbcS2i1 is 145 nt, 145 % 3 = 1, and it
  carries a stop in all three frames, so in those hosts an unspliced intron does not reduce
  expression — it destroys the protein.
- **Placement is abundant; usability is not.** 65 G|G sites in 906 nt, one every 13.9 nt. Of
  those, 16 give a clean orderable intron part and exactly **1** survives being embedded in an
  exon oligo.

## What this means for the page

The honest story is a **negative result reached by measurement**, and it is a better story
than a positive one would have been. The sequence of it:

1. The literature says codon optimisation alone is not enough in this host, and introns are
   the documented fix (Baier 2018: >100-fold mRNA, ~8-fold protein over a codon-optimised
   intron-free control).
2. The intron itself is compatible with our pipeline. We checked, and it passes unchanged.
3. The better intron is not available to us, for a reason we can show in one figure.
4. Placement is not the constraint. Ordering and assembly fidelity are.
5. On the enzyme the reaction actually uses, the backbone has already spent the budget.
6. So we left introns out, and we can say exactly what it would have cost.

That is an engineering cycle with a measured stop condition, which is worth more than a
feature. It also matches what Jan recommended on separate grounds — that intron choice and
spacing have no standardised database and would need literature work of their own.

## The figures

Two are built. The rest are ranked by what they add.

### Built

**1 — `01_intron_gc_band.svg`. "An intron has to pass the same GC rule as everything else we
order."** Two stacked GC traces over the 50 nt sliding window with the band drawn: rbcS2i1
inside it at every window, LHCBM1i2 breaking out at twelve. One image answers why the weaker
intron is the one we can use. Matrix-independent, so it cannot go stale.

**2 — `02_fidelity_waterfall.svg`. "At level 0 the backbone costs more than the introns do."**
Left panel, the running total from 1.000 down to 0.723. Right panel, a zoom on the strip where
the decision happens, with two arrows: room above the floor 0.0444, what three introns cost
0.0710. The second arrow is visibly longer than the first, which is the whole argument.

### Worth building next

**3 — the cliff.** Fidelity against architecture, with the forced intron count annotated on
each and the 0.75 floor drawn across. Shows 0.7234 / 0.1299 / 0.0812 and, crucially, that
14S and 19S are forced to more junctions by the 350 nt oligo cap rather than choosing them.
This is the figure that closes the question, and it needs a clear label that the counts are
minima, not preferences.

**4 — the host gate.** Six hosts against two questions: can it splice, and is there evidence
here. Three fail the first and two more fail the second, leaving one. Pair it with the
arithmetic — 145 nt, frameshift, stop in all three frames — so the consequence is concrete.
Small, fast to read, and shows the scope was thought about.

**5 — the funnel.** 65 G|G sites along the 9S coding sequence as a rug, with the 16 orderable
ones filled and the 3 chosen marked. Makes "placement is abundant, usability is not" visible
in one strip, and it is the natural companion to figure 3.

### Lower priority

**6 — seed spread.** Five seeds on one target: 0.1615, 0.1615, 0.5963, 0.7234, 0.7234, with
the floor drawn. The honesty figure — it shows we checked whether the headline was a lucky
draw. Worth including if there is room, because the spread is genuinely large.

**7 — the engineering cycle.** The two placement bugs measurement caught: repeated overhangs
scoring 0.18, then a count fixed from spacing picking the worst end of the curve. Good for a
judging narrative, but it needs careful framing so it reads as "the controls worked" rather
than "we shipped bugs". Prose may serve it better than a chart.

### Deliberately not building

- **The cross-host comparison.** The mechanism is cheaper to place in yeast and Arabidopsis,
  but those are exactly the hosts with no evidence that rbcS2i1 does anything. A chart makes
  the easy hosts look attractive and buries the caveat that runs opposite to it. This belongs
  in a sentence, not a figure.
- **Anything from `docs/intron_fidelity.md`'s tables as they stand.** Every figure in that
  document is on the wrong matrix and must not be redrawn until it is updated. Its header
  still reads "*Measured 2026-10-16*", which is the same day the matrix was corrected, so
  the date gives no warning that the numbers under it are stale.

## Format

The wiki has no charting library — `package.json` carries React, Tailwind and daisyUI and
nothing else — and `Figure.tsx` takes an `src`. So these go up as **static SVG** through the
iGEM uploads tool and are referenced from `static.igem.wiki`, the same path every other
figure uses.

SVG rather than PNG because the traces stay crisp at any zoom, which matters since every
picture on the wiki opens large through `ZoomablePhoto`. Text is kept as text
(`svg.fonttype: none`), so it stays selectable and sharp.

The palette is taken from the wiki's own theme in `src/containers/App/App.css` — primary
green `#65b93d`, secondary pink `#ff66c4`, accent cyan `#5cd6ff` — so the figures read as
part of the page. There is a precedent for hand-authored inline SVG in `RnaRail.tsx`, but
these are data-dense enough that generating them from the JSON is safer: a hand-placed
coordinate drifts from the measurement, a generated one cannot.

## One thing to settle before any fidelity number goes on the wiki

An independent pass over the enzyme/matrix pairing turned up a question the repository does
not answer, and it is larger than the intron work.

`design_oneshot` defaults to `enzyme="BsaI"` and `matrix="BsaI-HFv2"` (`design.py:360`,
`design.py:363`), and it wraps every fragment with BsaI sites — verified on a real design,
whose first oligo contains `GGTCTC` and no `GAAGAC`. As a pairing that is self-consistent:
the wrapper enzyme and the scoring table name the same enzyme.

But the same call passes `destination=DESTINATION_OVERHANGS["level0"]`, and
`assembly_spec.py:43` says a level 0 reaction is cut by BbsI and "must be scored against the
BbsI-HF matrix", adding that scoring it with BsaI-HFv2 "describes a reaction that does not
happen". `design.py` never imports `assembly_spec`, so that stage table is unreachable from
the one-shot path; the only consumer is `inventories.py:314`.

**Nothing in the repository states which enzyme cuts the one-shot tube, or defends
BsaI-HFv2 for it.** Both numbers are recorded side by side in `CLAUDE.md:402` without a
verdict. The consequence is that `0.828` is published as *the* fidelity in `README.md:63`,
`METHODS_ONE_PAGE.md:34` and `PPROPT_FOR_THE_WET_LAB.md:182` — the last of which also states
at line 133 that level 0 is BbsI.

On the same 9S overhang set the two tables give **0.8282** and **0.7928**, a difference of
**0.0353**. That matters beyond a decimal place: `docs/intron_fidelity.md:16` attributes a
cost of **0.026** to three introns, which is *smaller than the gap between the two matrices*.
A figure that put an intron-free BsaI-HFv2 number beside an intron-bearing BbsI-HF one would
be reporting the matrix change as if it were the intron cost, and the sign of the conclusion
would not be safe.

Every figure described above avoids this by scoring both ends of every comparison on
BbsI-HF, which is why figure 3 is titled "both ends scored on BbsI-HF". That keeps the intron
figures internally valid whichever way the one-shot question is settled. It does not settle
it, and the answer should be known before the wiki quotes a fidelity for PPROPT as a whole.

**This is a question for Jan**, and it is a different question from the one already answered.
He confirmed the level 0 *overhangs* and the vector. What is unresolved is which enzyme cuts
the reaction PPROPT's own one-shot designer is modelling.

Two smaller findings from the same pass, both out of scope here and neither touched:

- `tests/test_level1.py:134` asserts a BsaI-HFv2 score is below `0.751683`, which is a
  BbsI-HF figure, and draws a cross-stage conclusion from it. The assertion passes either
  way, so it is the conclusion rather than the test that crosses matrices. The same
  comparison is carried in prose at `assembly_spec.py:120`.
- `MAX_ORDERABLE_EXON_NT = 320` is exactly right for BbsI — measured wrapper overhead is 30
  nt, and 320 + 30 = 350 — but it is a hardcoded constant rather than one derived from
  `PART_ENZYME`. It is correct only because `PART_ENZYME` is BbsI. A BsaI wrapper is 28 nt
  and would allow 322.

## What these figures do not establish

- **Nothing here is measured at the bench.** Fidelity is a prediction from Pryor's
  mis-ligation data, not an assembly anyone ran. It says nothing about whether a spliced
  intron raises expression in a designed PPR, which nobody has tested.
- **One intron, one host, one target per architecture.** rbcS2i1 in *C. reinhardtii*
  nuclear throughout. LHCBM1i2 was tested only against the synthesis rules.
- **The floor is a judgement.** `FIDELITY_FLOOR = 0.75` is not a published threshold. The 9S
  result misses it by 0.0266, which is close enough that the figure should show the number
  rather than a verdict.
- **The comparison across architectures is not a comparison of placements.** 14S and 19S are
  forced to more junctions than 9S, so their lower scores measure the ordering constraint as
  much as the biology.
