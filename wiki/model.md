## What we model, and what we do not

We model **manufacturability**, not biology. Given a protein we want, this project asks which
of the many DNA sequences encoding it a synthesis company can actually make, and how
confidently the resulting fragments will assemble in the right order.

We do **not** model binding affinity, expression level, or any cellular outcome. Nothing on
this page predicts whether a PPR protein will grip its RNA. That is a wet-lab question and we
have not answered it.

Stating this up front matters because the project's headline result is a *refusal*, and a
refusal is only meaningful if the thing being refused is clearly defined.

## 1 · The design model is deterministic

The PPR code maps each RNA base to a repeat, so the protein follows from the target by lookup.
There is no search and no scoring at this stage, and therefore no uncertainty to model.
Everything genuinely modelled in this project happens after the protein exists.

## 2 · The object under test is the ordered fragment, not the gene

This is the decision the rest of the page rests on.

A synthesis order is not the coding sequence. It is the coding sequence **plus** an entry
overhang and an enzyme wrapper bolted onto each end — fixed, GC-rich, and not ours to change.
For a long time our constraints were evaluated against the coding span alone, while the thing
that would be manufactured was something slightly different and measurably harder.

Evaluating the contract on what leaves the building, rather than on what we designed, is what
turned a passing project into a refusing one.

## 3 · The GC band, and where it came from

Our synthesis policy enforces local GC within a fixed band over a sliding window, as a hard
constraint.

We traced the provenance of that band and the answer is uncomfortable, so we are stating it
plainly: **nobody recorded choosing it.** It appears in the project's initial commit, filed
under this project's own choices rather than among the constants with cited sources. It
happens to match one vendor's published codon-optimisation guidance exactly, and we enforce it
*more strictly* than that vendor states it.

[[FIG: the band's numeric range and window length | src/ppropt/policy.py]]

We compared our fragments against every vendor rule we could verify first-hand rather than
assume. [[FIG: how many published vendor rules our refused fragments actually violate |
docs/vendor_rule_comparison.md]]

That comparison is the honest context for the refusal: our own policy is the binding
constraint, not any manufacturer's stated limit.

## 4 · Secondary structure, measured rather than assumed

Vendor guidance describes GC as a proxy for secondary structure. We had been enforcing the
proxy without ever measuring the thing it stands for.

So we measured it: every fragment folded with ViennaRNA under DNA parameters, scored against
the one published hairpin threshold we could find.

[[FIG: how many fragments breach the threshold, out of how many, and which fragment position
they cluster in | docs/vendor_rule_comparison.md §3b]]

Two findings worth carrying into the wiki text:

- The breaches **cluster in one fragment position**, and trace to the PPR repeat array's own
  self-similarity — a property of the protein we are designing, not of the codons we chose.
- Fixing GC is **structure-neutral**. It neither helps nor harms folding.
  [[FIG: median ΔG shift between the two arms | docs/vendor_rule_comparison.md §3b]]

A methodological note that cost us a wrong result: ViennaRNA's energy model is **global
process state**. Loading DNA parameters inside one arm of an experiment meant the other arm
folded under RNA rules, and the two arms were silently incomparable. The corrected measurement
differs from the first by a wide margin. [[FIG: the two ΔG values for the same 48-mer under
RNA versus DNA parameters | validation/experiments/wrapper_aware_pilot.py]]

## 5 · The 200-design panel

Every experiment on this page is run against one frozen panel of designs, generated by this
software from random target sequences and pinned so that each experiment measures the same
set. They are synthetic, not biological targets, and the panel is covered by a recorded hash
so a later run cannot quietly measure something else.

## 6 · The wrapper-aware constraint, and its negative control

The candidate fix is a single change: show the codon optimiser the **whole ordered fragment**
— wrapper included — rather than only the coding span, then let it choose different synonymous
spellings for the same protein.

[[FIG: orderable fragments before and after, and the number of hard findings removed |
PANEL_WRAPPER_AWARE_2026-10-11.md §1]]

**The control is the load-bearing part of this result**, and deserves to be in the wiki text
rather than buried: with the one change removed, the software reproduced the original
sequences exactly, base for base, across the whole panel. That is what makes the improvement
attributable to the change rather than to anything else that moved.

[[FIG: control result | PANEL_WRAPPER_AWARE_2026-10-11.md §2]]

Cost in codon quality is small but not zero, and a few designs improved.
[[FIG: median and worst CAI cost, and how many designs improved | PANEL_WRAPPER_AWARE §3]]

**It is not switched on.** A strong panel result is evidence, not a decision: enabling it
would move every number this project has published.

### A correction we are keeping on the record

An earlier revision of this experiment reported a batch of failures and a confident
explanation for them. That explanation was wrong — the failures were a defect in our own test
harness, not a property of those targets. Once fixed, the result changed substantially. The
write-up keeps the incorrect claim visible rather than quietly replacing it, because a
methods section that only ever reports things working is not a methods section.

## 7 · What we tried that did not work

**Tightening the repeat-uniqueness window.** Internal repetition in the DNA is a real
synthesis risk, so we tested a stricter setting. It reduced the longest repeat only slightly,
left the overall repeat burden essentially unchanged, and cost substantially more in codon
quality. [[FIG: longest repeat before/after, burden, and CAI cost | the k-mer experiment]]

The reason is structural: the repeat burden is a property of the PPR architecture itself —
tandem near-identical repeats are what a PPR protein *is* — not of the codons chosen to spell
it. Re-spelling cannot remove it. We report the negative result rather than dropping it.

## 8 · What none of this establishes

- No sequence was ever submitted to a vendor, so no vendor has accepted or refused anything.
- Folding is a prediction from a published energy model, not a measurement, and not any
  particular vendor's implementation.
- Predicted assembly fidelity comes from published ligation-count matrices, not from a
  reaction anyone ran.
- The whole-fragment folding figure has **no published threshold** to score against and is
  reported for completeness only.
- None of it is evidence that a designed PPR binds its target.
